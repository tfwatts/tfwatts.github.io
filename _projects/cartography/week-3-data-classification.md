## Overview

This layout presents the Island of Hawaiʻi through four complementary maps built on a shared 10-meter shaded-relief base: a shaded-relief map with a hypsometric elevation tint, a satellite imagery basemap, mean annual temperature, and the geologic age of surface lava flows. Together they show how the island’s volcanic terrain governs both its climate, cool summits grading to a warm coast, and its surface, assembled over hundreds of thousands of years of eruptions. The assignment’s purpose was to demonstrate core cartographic design principles such as figure-ground, visual hierarchy, and a balanced layout while applying an appropriate color scheme to each data type: a hypsometric tint for elevation, a sequential ramp for temperature, and a qualitative palette for lava-flow age. Each thematic layer is blended at partial transparency so the underlying hillshade shows through, integrating terrain with theme across all four panels.
